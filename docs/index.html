The problem

CAD nests boxes. You pay for the material left on the table.

Bounding boxes throw away the gaps between real shapes: two L-brackets or a ring that would interlock perfectly are rejected because their rectangles collide. And proving that two contours never touch is an expensive geometric test no sequential loop can run hundreds of thousands of times per second.

The usual outcome: 30–50% of every sheet lost, manual rework, and parts that only almost clear each other in production.

Bounding box

A valid interlock is discarded and the gap is wasted.

True shape

The real contour takes the gap and keeps the separation exact.

What it does

One engine, from raw import to the cut file.

Everything is measured in real millimeters. Nothing is simulated: the geometry that gets evaluated is the geometry that gets exported.

01

True-shape intake

DXF with topological stitching (LINE, ARC, SPLINE, LWPOLYLINE), DWG through ODA, SVG with transforms and curves, vector PDF, and rasters with silhouette detection.

02

Holes and islands

Contour hierarchy is preserved: a ring only occupies its crown, not its box, and interior islands can be nested too.

03

GPU evaluation

A 2048×1024 ping-pong buffer, up to 8 parts per pass, 96 samples per part, and shader-side pruning of pixels that cannot win.

04

Slide to contact

Every candidate slides until it touches the geometry already placed, so the clearance is exact instead of pixel-approximate.

05

Sheets or roll

Multi-sheet layouts, or a continuous roll that grows the occupancy grid incrementally instead of rounding length up.

06

Production output

DXF with closed polylines on a CutContour layer ($INSUNITS=4) and imposition PDF with cut marks and bleed.

Inside the engine

Pixels decide. Geometry verifies.

The sheet and every part are rasterized into float textures where depth encodes free space. A candidate position is scored by its weakest sample, not its average, so a single collision is enough to reject it. Survivors are then slid to contact with the real polygon data, and only exact geometry reaches the export.

  • Evaluation grid2048 × 1024 px
  • Parts per GPU pass8
  • Samples per part96 (64 edge + 32 interior)
  • Rotation step1°
  • Batch read cost3.78 ms
  • Collision checkexact polygons, 0.000000 mm²

Pipeline

Five steps, no manual touch-up.

Contours

Each file becomes polygons in millimeters, with holes normalized to their box.

Rasterize

Sheet and parts are drawn into float textures; depth encodes the free space.

Score

The shader scores thousands of positions per part and angle, using edge and interior samples.

Search

A genetic search picks order, rotation and position; a fine window refines the best candidates.

Export

Solutions are compacted, clearance is verified, and DXF plus PDF are written.

Usage

Start it, drop parts, export.

  1. 1Import the partsDrop DXF, DWG, SVG, PDF or images. The app shows detected vertices and holes before nesting starts.
  2. 2Set the runSheets or roll, size, spacing, bleed, copies per reference and rotation step.
  3. 3Nest and exportThe engine runs in a hidden GPU worker and reports every generation. When it stops, export DXF 1:1 and the imposition PDF.
